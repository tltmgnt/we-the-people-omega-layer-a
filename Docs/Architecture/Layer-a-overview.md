# Layer A — Foundational architecture

## Purpose
Establish constitutional, legal, and democratic conditions for all WE THE PEOPLE Ω systems. Institutional efficiency, popularity, security, or economic benefit cannot independently justify bypassing applicable constitutional constraints.

## Constitutional and legal foundations
The U.S. Constitution and amendments, applicable statutes, regulations, treaties, binding judicial decisions, and jurisdiction-specific legal authority inform review. Tribal sovereignty and government-to-government relationships require distinct treatment.

## Seven domains
A1 Rights and Liberties; A2 Rule of Law; A3 Elections and Representation; A4 Courts and Justice; A5 Ethics and Integrity; A6 Federalism; A7 Tribal Sovereignty. See the [seven domain checklists](../Domains/README.md) for proposed review questions, evidence requests and escalation triggers.

See the [assurance gate](../Assurance/review-gate.md) for proposed statuses, responsibilities and validation.

## Assurance workflow
1. Define the proposal, actor, jurisdictions, date, and scope.
2. Identify applicable authority and unresolved questions.
3. Assess affected rights and protected interests.
4. Check required procedure, fairness, and review mechanisms.
5. Confirm institutional and jurisdictional boundaries, including Tribal authority where applicable.
6. Document accountability, remedies, sources, and uncertainty.
7. Escalate serious unresolved issues to qualified human legal review.
8. Version findings and preserve challenges and corrections.

## Limits
This is a review checklist and research architecture, not a substitute for jurisdiction-specific legal advice. No automated approval may substitute for a legally required decision by an authorized actor.

## Foundational principle
Constitutional legitimacy is a prerequisite to lawful action, not a score that can be traded against performance gains.
