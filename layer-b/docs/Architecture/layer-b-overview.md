# Layer B — architecture and mission

## Purpose and boundaries

Establish a unified framework for examining how authorized institutions turn mandates
and resources into public services. Layer A defines constitutional legitimacy, rights,
jurisdiction and public accountability. Layer B examines operating plans, budgets, workforce,
systems, assets, delivery and corrective action within those boundaries.

The six domains are analytical categories, not replacement departments or new authority.
Intergovernmental coordination preserves each participant's obligations; Tribal sovereignty
requires distinct Layer A assessment. General public-governance references inform research,
not a universal legal standard across jurisdictions.

## Ten strategic objectives

| Objective | Operational intent |
| --- | --- |
| Operational clarity | Identify owners, responsibilities, dependencies and escalation |
| Fiscal traceability | Link authorized funding, allocations, commitments, payments, assets and outcomes |
| Procurement integrity | Improve competition, documentation, delivery verification and management |
| Workforce capacity | Align staffing, skills, training and institutional knowledge with demand |
| Records integrity | Maintain reliable, accessible and appropriately protected action records |
| Service reliability | Improve availability, quality, accessibility and continuity |
| Asset stewardship | Manage the full infrastructure lifecycle |
| Administrative efficiency | Reduce avoidable waiting, duplication and rework while preserving safeguards |
| Independent accountability | Make findings reproducible, reviewable and correctable |
| Continuous improvement | Convert verified lessons into durable procedures, training and resource decisions |

Balance effectiveness, legality, quality, fairness, resilience and public value. Do not
maximize speed or minimize cost in isolation.

## Operational lifecycle

| Stage | Analysis and deliverable |
| --- | --- |
| MANDATE | Purpose, authority, jurisdiction, obligations and accountable institution |
| PLANNING | Demand, cost, workforce, capital, dependencies and funding constraints |
| READINESS | Personnel, training, facilities, records, technology, procurement and procedures |
| DELIVERY | Actions, handoffs, costs and service outcomes |
| VERIFICATION | Quality, expenditure, compliance questions, acceptance, access and evidence integrity |
| CORRECTION | Errors, backlogs, audit findings, service failures and recovery |
| LEARNING | Revised procedures, assumptions, training, contracts and measures |

Map formal stages and actual relationships among people, systems, budgets and providers.
For each connection record input, output, owner, dependency, failure mode and escalation.
Operational assurance feeds verified lessons back into planning and delivery.

## Definition of success

A participating institution can demonstrate documented authority and responsibility,
traceable funding and deliverables, reviewable procurement, workforce capacity matched
to demand, trustworthy protected records, accessible recoverable services, measured
end-to-end burden, independently verified corrections and improvements that persist.
Report uncertainty, limitations and counter-metrics with every claimed result.
