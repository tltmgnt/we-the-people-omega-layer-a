# B3 — Procurement and contracts

## Objective

Examine whether purchases are authorized, appropriately competed, managed and verified against delivered goods, services or works.

These are proposed operational review prompts. Establish applicability with Layer A;
do not infer legal authority or actual institutional performance from checklist completion.

## Research modules

- Need justification and specification quality
- Competition and award transparency
- Vendor eligibility and conflicts of interest
- Contract terms, deliverables and acceptance criteria
- Change orders, amendments and scope growth
- Milestone verification and invoice reconciliation
- Supplier performance and dependency risk
- Contract closeout, records and remedies

## Review questions

- What rules apply to this jurisdiction, funding source, method and contract type?
- Do acceptance evidence and invoices reconcile with the contract baseline and approved changes?
- Are competition exceptions explained by the applicable process and market context?

## Candidate measures

- Competition and exception rates by procurement type
- Awards with documented evaluation records
- Change-order value and frequency
- On-time delivery and verified acceptance
- Invoice discrepancy and correction rates
- Contract closeout timeliness
- Supplier concentration and continuity risk
- Independent audit and verified corrective-action closure

Use the [indicator specification](../Methods/scorecard.md): definitions, denominators,
periods, exclusions, uncertainty, owner and revision. Pair speed/cost with quality,
access, safety and rework measures.

## Evidence and escalation

Request relevant authorized process maps, dated authority references, financial or service
records, verification results and responsible-owner attestations. Record missing information,
competing explanations and data restrictions. Unresolved authority or rights issues return
to Layer A review; operational uncertainty remains visible in the Layer B record.

## Interpretation safeguard

Do not assume one procurement rulebook applies to every federal, state, local or intergovernmental purchase.

## Procurement lifecycle

Need → specification → solicitation → evaluation → award → contract baseline → performance → change control → invoice verification → payment → audit and learning.
