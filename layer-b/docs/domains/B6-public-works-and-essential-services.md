# B6 — Public works and essential services

## Objective

Evaluate whether infrastructure and essential services are safe, reliable, accessible, resilient and maintained at sustainable lifecycle cost.

These are proposed operational review prompts. Establish applicability with Layer A;
do not infer legal authority or actual institutional performance from checklist completion.

## Research modules

- Asset inventory and condition assessment
- Capital planning and project delivery
- Preventive and corrective maintenance
- Utilities and essential-service continuity
- Roads, sidewalks and public facilities
- Waste, water, drainage and environmental services
- Contractor and supplier coordination
- Emergency repair and recovery
- Lifecycle finance and deferred-maintenance management

## Review questions

- What asset, supplier, staffing and funding dependencies determine continuity?
- Do reported low costs omit deferred maintenance or future recovery liabilities?
- Can people report faults, and do inspections corroborate complaint trends?

## Candidate measures

- Asset condition and maintenance backlog
- Availability and outage duration
- Repair time and repeat-failure rate
- Project cost and schedule variance
- Accessibility and service coverage
- Preventive maintenance completion
- Recovery time after disruption
- Lifecycle cost and deferred maintenance
- Complaints paired with verified inspections

Use the [indicator specification](../Methods/scorecard.md): definitions, denominators,
periods, exclusions, uncertainty, owner and revision. Pair speed/cost with quality,
access, safety and rework measures.

## Evidence and escalation

Request relevant authorized process maps, dated authority references, financial or service
records, verification results and responsible-owner attestations. Record missing information,
competing explanations and data restrictions. Unresolved authority or rights issues return
to Layer A review; operational uncertainty remains visible in the Layer B record.

## Interpretation safeguard

Low complaint counts do not establish good service: reporting barriers and loss of trust can suppress complaints.
